Case 01 · Platform Transformation

Core AI Group PM, DIY & Expert Experiences

Self-serve, assisted, and AI-assisted stopped being separate products.

A customer doing their own taxes or books hit a wall and had to leave the product to get help — a different interface, a different context, a different wait. I led the work that dissolved that boundary: help and AI arrive inside the flow the customer is already in. The mechanism was an AI-first expert platform and the shared layer underneath it, but the outcome was measured on the consumer side — fewer abandoned sessions, fewer handoffs that felt like starting over.

Scope: tax and bookkeeping first — the highest-stakes, most abandonment-prone DIY journeys — then payroll. Delivered in a regulated domain, where every change clears compliance, security, and legal review before a customer sees it.

Recommendation work from this platform is patent pending.

The bet

Everyone else was adding AI to the tools experts already had. We argued for taking the tools away.

A dozen surfaces, each a little smarter, still leaves an expert doing the integration work in their head. Collapsing them into one assistant meant accepting a harder build, a slower start, and a real chance of being wrong — in exchange for the only version of this that compounds.

How I led it · four decisions

One assistant on the surface. Four decisions that made it possible.

01.a Framing Assistant, not another tool The organizing decision — one assistant surface, not a dozen point tools bolted onto the existing workspace.
Problem framingScope
01.a

Adding AI to every existing tool would have multiplied the surfaces experts already juggled. Consolidating to one assistant meant fewer, better entry points — and a platform bet instead of a feature bet.

What we chose not to build mattered more than what we did

01.b Platform Build once, serve anywhere Capabilities built as portable services rather than features welded to a single screen.
ReusePortability
01.b

The expensive mistake in assistant work is rebuilding the same capability per surface. I pushed for a shared layer so a capability written once could serve the expert workspace, adjacent internal surfaces, and third-party applications alike.

The same bet that made third-party distribution possible

01.c Model The expert supervises the AI Inverting the workflow — AI drafts, the expert reviews, corrects, and coaches.
AI draftsCoaching
01.c

Copilot patterns keep the human typing. Inverting it makes the expert a reviewer and teacher, which is where their judgment is worth the most. We proved it on a narrow, high-frequency, easily-measured workflow before generalizing.

Narrow proving ground first, generalize second

01.d Quality Nothing ships without a bar A two-layer evaluation practice — human review paired with automated judging — as a release gate, not a report.
Human evalAuto eval
01.d

Assistant quality is unfalsifiable without a measurement system, so evaluation was funded as product work, not QA. Human ratings calibrated the automated judge; the judge made continuous measurement affordable.

Evaluation as product, not QA

Case 02 · Conversational AI for SMB

Core AI Cross-functional partner / sponsor

Intuit Intelligence for small business.

The customer-facing counterpart to the expert work. I partnered across the customer-facing AI organization to align its capability catalog and quality standards with the expert platform's — so the same capability behaved consistently whether a small-business owner asked or an expert did.

The problem

Two AI organizations were converging on the same capabilities from different directions, with different definitions of "good."

The play

Use the expert capability catalog as the shared reference for governance and quality, rather than negotiating each capability twice.

The outcome

Cross-platform coherence — a broad set of capabilities shipped to general availability, held to a quality bar both sides agreed to.

Case 03 · Third-party AI distribution

Core AI GPM, Strategic Sponsor

Intuit inside ChatGPT & Claude.

The external payoff of the portable-capability bet. Bringing TurboTax and QuickBooks into the AI assistants customers already use — extending reach beyond owned surfaces and into the ambient AI layer.

Claude connector · live demo

The bet

Customers will increasingly start jobs-to-be-done from a generic AI chat surface. We need to be where they are — not just where we own the URL.

The design

Capabilities built headless and platform-agnostic, so the same one could run on an owned surface or inside someone else's assistant.

The proof

Working connectors for TurboTax and QuickBooks running inside Claude and ChatGPT, held to the same evaluation standard as our owned surfaces.

Case 04 · Operating model

Working code Multi-agent

The Dependency Planner.

Applying AI to how my own teams work. An AI-native planning tool I built end-to-end. Stakeholders submit dependency requests, Claude triages them, scores them, surfaces risks, and pushes to Jira on acceptance — turning a flooded intake queue into a governed, ranked plan in seconds.

Intelligent triage

Claude scores every request 0–100, assigns priority, routes to the right capability, and flags risks.

Document extraction

Paste a PRD or deck and the form auto-populates.

On-demand re-evaluation

Refresh rationale and risks as context shifts.

Planning Advisor chat

Ask anything about the roadmap from any item page.

Push to Jira

Accepted items create tickets with fields pre-populated.

Deck generation

Executive-ready slides from live plan data.

Now part of how the team plans: intake, triage, and prioritization run through it every cycle, so PMs spend their time on judgment calls instead of queue triage. Built with Claude Code for triage and chat, document connectors for Drive / Figma / Slack, and a Jira integration on accept.

Venture 02 · 2018 – 2019

Co-founder Vertical SaaS

Fitify — a CRM for fitness studios.

An all-in-one studio management platform — scheduling, check-in, point of sale, payroll, and reporting — built as a direct competitor to Mindbody. The full operating system for a boutique fitness business.

The wedge

Mindbody owned the market but studios complained about clunky tooling. Fitify rebuilt the daily front-desk workflow around speed.

The product

Scheduler, visual class layouts, check-in, POS with inventory and class series, time clock, and reporting — one platform, end-to-end.

The lesson

Vertical SaaS lives or dies on the boring operational surface. The bike-grid check-in was the feature that won demos.
