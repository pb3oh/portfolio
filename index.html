AI agent · Chrome extension

Curator

An analyst, not an executor.

What it gave me

A chance to build something entirely on my own, and to bring AI into a space that genuinely interests me.

Serious collectors scroll through thousands of listings to find the few worth buying. Curator cuts 40,000+ listings a week down to the ~50 that deserve a human's attention. It runs deterministic checks first (seller history, cert validity, price against comps, image count), then a vision-enabled Claude reasoner scores each listing and explains why.

The rule I built it around: the agent recommends, the person decides. It never bids or buys on its own, and every decision the person makes becomes training data. A label firewall keeps those decisions out of the agent's inputs, so the evals measure the model rather than the answers it was shown.

Consumer · benefits guidance

Cairn

A trail of markers through the Medicare maze.

What it gave me

A problem I heard firsthand from my parents as they retired. Nobody was solving it well, and it clearly needed a product.

Retirees leave real money unclaimed because Medicare, state prescription programs, property tax credits, and VA benefits each have their own rules, forms, and deadlines. Cairn is a 4-minute review: eleven plain-English questions run against 14+ federal, state, and county eligibility rules and return a personalized report with apply links.

It also handles the people who haven't enrolled yet. It computes their enrollment window, flags late-enrollment penalties, and generates field-by-field guides for each application. Eight states are seeded with detailed program data; the rest fall back to federal coverage.

Internal tool · AI triage

The Dependency Planner

AI applied to how my own teams plan.

What it gave me

Hands-on proof that the tooling I ask teams to adopt can change how a product org runs, starting with mine.

Stakeholders submit dependency requests, and Claude triages them: it scores each one from 0 to 100, assigns priority, surfaces risks and open questions, and pushes accepted items to Jira. Paste in a PRD and the intake form fills itself; a planning-advisor chat answers questions about the roadmap. A flooded intake queue becomes a ranked plan, and PMs spend their time on judgment calls instead of sorting the queue.

Intuit · DIY & Expert Experiences

Reinventing the Intuit Expert Platform as AI-native.

Led end to end, and accountable for AI adoption.

What it gave me

My first corporate chapter, and the opportunity to build AI experiences at scale and to work with AI tooling every day.

The Intuit Expert Platform dates back to 2020, the year I joined Intuit. By 2025, its tax and bookkeeping experts were working across a dozen tools, doing the integration work in their heads. Over 2025 and 2026 I led the effort to rebuild the platform as AI-native, end to end, and I was accountable for the outcome that mattered most: whether experts actually adopted the AI.

The copilot was one of the core changes, but not the only one. Just as important was how AI showed up everywhere else in the product, and the infrastructure and tooling that let us ship it safely. All of it shipped in a regulated domain, where every change clears compliance, security, and legal review.

What my team owned

  1. 01 · Copilot

    A copilot in the workspace

    The AI drafts; the expert reviews, corrects, and coaches. We proved it on one narrow, high-frequency workflow before generalizing.

  2. 02 · Adoption

    AI woven into the UI

    Orchestrating how AI experiences surface across the platform, inside the flows experts already use, so adoption was designed in rather than hoped for.

  3. 03 · Infrastructure

    The tooling underneath

    A shared capability layer any surface can call, and an evaluation practice (human ratings calibrating an automated judge) that gates every release.

The bet

Everyone else was adding AI to the tools experts already had. We argued for rebuilding the platform around it.

A dozen surfaces, each a little smarter, still leave an expert doing the integration work in their head. Going AI-native meant a harder build and a slower start, in exchange for the only version of this that compounds.
