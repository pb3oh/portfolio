Intuit · 2020 – present

Group PM, DIY & Expert Experiences

The AI-native Intuit Expert Platform.

Someone doing their own taxes or books would hit a wall and have to leave the product to get help: a different interface, a different context, a different wait. I led the work that removed that boundary, so help and AI arrive inside the flow the customer is already in.

The mechanism was an AI-first expert platform and the shared capability layer underneath it. The outcome was measured on the customer side: fewer abandoned sessions, and fewer handoffs that felt like starting over. We started with tax and bookkeeping, the highest-stakes and most abandonment-prone journeys, then extended to payroll. All of it shipped in a regulated domain, where every change clears compliance, security, and legal review.

Recommendation work from this platform is patent pending. Details generalized where under NDA.

The bet

Everyone else was adding AI to the tools experts already had. We argued for taking the tools away.

A dozen surfaces, each a little smarter, still leave an expert doing the integration work in their head. Collapsing them into one assistant meant a harder build, a slower start, and a real chance of being wrong, in exchange for the only version of this that compounds.

How I led it

One assistant on the surface. Four decisions underneath it.

01 · Framing

An assistant, not another tool

Adding AI to every existing tool would have multiplied the surfaces experts already juggled. One assistant meant fewer, better entry points: a platform bet instead of a feature bet.

What we didn't build mattered most

02 · Platform

Build once, serve anywhere

Capabilities built as portable services, not features welded to one screen, so the same capability serves the expert workspace, adjacent surfaces, and third-party assistants.

No rebuilding per surface

03 · Model

The expert supervises the AI

We inverted the copilot pattern: the AI drafts, and the expert reviews, corrects, and coaches, which is where their judgment is worth the most. We proved it on one narrow, high-frequency workflow first.

Narrow proving ground, then generalize

04 · Quality

Nothing ships without a bar

A two-layer evaluation practice as the release gate: human ratings calibrate an automated judge, and the judge makes continuous measurement affordable.

Evaluation funded as product, not QA

What carries over

Principles I build AI-native products by.

01

Agentic workflows over monolithic prompts.

Real work breaks down into roles, tools, and handoffs. Specialized agents plan, retrieve, act, and verify, with deterministic checkpoints between the probabilistic steps.

02

Governance by design, not an audit afterthought.

Evals, policy gates, and lineage live next to the agent graph. If it can't be measured and attested, it doesn't ship to regulated customers.

03

The expert stays in the loop.

The most valuable AI doesn't replace the professional; it compounds them. Confidence over autonomy, traceability over magic.

2018 – 2019 Co-founder Vertical SaaS

Fitify: a CRM for fitness studios.

An all-in-one studio management platform built to compete with Mindbody: scheduling, check-in, point of sale, payroll, and reporting. It was the complete operating system for a boutique fitness business.

The wedge

Mindbody owned the market, but studios complained about clunky tooling. Fitify rebuilt the daily front-desk workflow around speed.

The product

Scheduler, visual class layouts, check-in, POS with inventory and class series, time clock, and reporting in one platform.

The lesson

Vertical SaaS lives or dies on the boring operational surface. The bike-grid check-in was the feature that won demos.
